Tricky CSF Conflict Checks

By Měryem al Ixbilǐa
meryemsca@gmail.com

Published 21 September 2026

This is less of a fully-fledged article and more of a demonstration of how I do conflict checks, with particular attention to the tricky cases I had to work out the logic of myself. I hope it’s helpful!

I’m writing for readers who are familiar with charge group theory, SENA’s conflict rules, how armory is coded in the O&A, and using the complex search form to eliminate anything with an SC or multiple DCs from the item you’re checking. This is the method of conflict-checking used in Beatrice Domenici della Campana’s complex search conflict checking class and Marie de Blois’s handout on conflict checking with the Complex Search Form. I learned to use the complex search form from Beatrice (by taking her class and asking a lot of follow-up questions while I did an absurd amount of conflict checks), so if you’re familiar with her approach, you’ll understand what I’m doing.

Example: Mixed-Tincture Primary Charge Groups

Consider the armory Sable, in pale a bezant and a plate. How would this be coded?

  • SA
  • FIELD:sable:solid
  • ROUNDEL:2:g2pa:multicolor light:pl:unc:~ argent:~and or
  • INPALE:2:g2pa:multicolor light:~ argent:~and or
What changes can we make to this armory, how much difference would they be worth, and how can we code for them?
  • The field: a divided field is an SC and a different tincture is a DC, so the first line as SA.
