How to Comment

What to Say

Here are some articles that explain how commentary works and what kind of comments are helpful:

  • Intro to Kingdom-level Commentary in OSCAR by Muirenn ingen Dúnadaig
  • Commenting on Letters of Intent by Brunissende Dragonette
  • An OSCAR Commentary Checklist by Mathghamhain Ua Ruadháin
  • Commenting in OSCAR by Estelle de la Mer (page 81 of the Middle Kingdom Pursuivant’s Handbook)
  • Critique and Commentary: Tips and Tricks from Art School by Nest verch Gwilim

Currently, every kingdom has an internal letter posted on OSCAR, with a commentary period lasting one month in most kingdoms. (The internal letters for Caid and the West are only visible to in-kingdom commenters.) This lets us improve documentation, redraw if needed, return obvious conflicts, etc. with a shorter turnaround time. Kingdoms shouldn’t return items unless there’s a specific rule or precedent to return them under, so issues like presumption usually won’t get a response at kingdom, but it’s still helpful to mention them.

Technical Details

Commentary happens on OSCAR. You can make an account at https://oscar.sca.org/newuser.php and will need an account to get commentary permissions. Per Section VII of the Administrative Handbook, Principal Heralds should have a published, written policy regarding their kingdom’s standards for nomination as commenters. The policy will vary by kingdom, but usually you email your principal herald or their deputy and ask for commentary permissions. If this isn’t how it’s done, they’ll know what you should do instead.

In most kingdoms, you start by getting permission for internal commentary, then ask for external commentary privileges when you have a good track record. Some kingdoms will let out-of-kingdom commenters with kingdom commentary permissions comment on their letters, which you can find at Active Kingdom Letters

Once you have an OSCAR account, take a look at the Preferences page! You should make sure comments are displayed and that you’ll be notified of responses to your comments. You might also want to tweak the font settings; I have the font set to sans so it uses my browser’s preferred sans-serif font. For more information on how OSCAR works, see Using Laurel’s Online System for Commentary and Response (OSCAR) by Elisabetta Tommaso di Carduci.

Frequently-cited Appendix H sources and how to check citations in them:

  • Many sources have links in Appendix H.
  • FamilySearch:
    • FamilySearch requires a free account to view records.
    • The “batch number” can be found by expanding Document Information on the left and looking for the indexing batch. If there isn’t a batch number or it’s not an approved batch, check if an image of the original record was provided; if not, is the image available?
    • Using FamilySearch Historical Records by Domhnall na Moicheirghe and Juliana de Luna
