Introduction

The names in this article are Aragonese documentary forms of the names of men from the Jewish aljama (self-governing community) of Zaragoza, recorded in a legal document from 1331 establishing taxes within the aljama. This document was edited and published by Gunnar Tilander as “Documento desconocido de la aljama de Zaragoza del año 1331” [“Undiscovered document from the aljama of Zaragoza from the year 1331”] in Archivo de Filología Aragonesa, 50 (1994) pp. 351–397, Institución Fernando el Católico (C.S.I.C.) de la Excelentísima Diputación de Zaragoza, I.S.S.N. 0210-5624.

The full title of the document is Instrumentum publicum revocacionis sisie aljame judeorum Cesarauguste in omnibus et specialiter in vino et carnibus. 1331 [Public instrument regarding the revocation of the sisa (levy) imposed on the Jewish aljama of Zaragoza, covering all items, and specifically wine and meat. 1331], with the subtitle Per istud instrumentum judey aljame Cesarauguste inponunt sibi legem quod in victualibus que directe vel indirecte emunt seu emi posint per cristianos nullatenus posent ponere sissam, et si facerent incurrent penam XXti millium solidorum; fuit recognitum per juratos die VIIIª febroari anno MCCCX et IIIº [By means of this instrument, the aljama of the Jews of Zaragoza imposes upon itself a rule that they may in no way levy a tax on provisions which they buy or could buy directly or indirectly from Christians, and should they do so, they shall incur a penalty of twenty thousand solidi; this was acknowledged by the jurors on the 8th day of February in the year 1313]. Tilander notes that the true date of the document is 1331; the date of 1313 is a scribal error. The date of 8 February 1331 at the beginning of the document probably refers to the date of the resolution of the jurors to impose these taxes; the date of 17 November 1331 at the end of the document refers to its subsequent annulation.

From Tilander’s introduction:

The document contains statues of the aljama of Zaragoza, which in 1331 established certain levies which the aljama was required to pay: […]

This decree was confirmed by King James II, dando nos a entender al dito sennyor Rey que la dita ordinacion o imposicion no era cuentra fuero ni tocava en alguna cosa a la dita ciudad ni a los cristianos habitantes en aquella, segun nuestro cuydar [making it understood to the said Lord King that the said ordinance or levy was not contrary to the fuero, nor did it in any way affect the said city or the Christians inhabiting it, according to our understanding] (§ 36.6–11), but the jurors of Zaragoza protested, holding that it was against the Fuero of Aragón and prejudicial for the city, and they demanded that it be revoked (§ 36.11–20). The Jews therefore annulled the tax on loans, wine, meat and other wares, maintaining only the tax on drinkable and movable goods (§ 36.20–70).

[Fritz] Baer offers a similar document from Huesca from the year 1340 ([Die Juden im christlichen Spanien] pp. 286–293), and despite the renunciation of the levy on meat and wine in our document, a document from the aljama of Zaragoza from the year 1397, reproduced by Baer (pp. 727–732), corroborates this very levy, establishing excommunication on the Jews who don’t pay it.

Notes

Three people are mentioned multiple times, and I count every instance of their names separately: Barcellya, corredor publico = Barçellya, notario; Bitas Faleua = Bitas Fallyeua; Salamon Abnarrabj = Salamon Abnarrabj.

There are a few individuals who I think are not Jewish:

  • Domingo Pereç de Mallyen, notario publico de la ciudat de Çaragoça (1 instance)
  • Garcia Aznarez delos nauarros (1 instance)
  • Guillyem Veguer, clerigo missacantano (1 instance)
  • Steuan Gil (4 instances) = Steuan Gil Tarin (2 instances)

Except for the list of place names, their names are not included in the lists, but these names are still interesting (and my identification could be incorrect).

Header forms are attested names, with i/j and u/v swaps to follow modern expectations that these be separate letters. The Aben- surnames all use b for consistency.

Tilander notes a number of interesting features of the document’s orthography (numbers are the section and line number):

Vacillation between c and ç: centennial 8.1, çentenal 8.3; aducir 12.27, aduçir 9.1, 10.2; diciendo 12.13, diçiendo 9.3; facer 36.22, façer 1.5, etc. This vacillation had caused c to also appear before a, o, u: cabeca 27.26, 36.1,41 (next to cabeça 2.1), Acach 1.10,12 (next to Açach 1.9,12, etc.), fianca 10.15,16, carrar 12.24, racon 9.1,10, 10.8, 27.36,40, 36.57, Curi 1.17, Caragoça 1.2, 16.1,8,21.

Vacillation between g and gu […]

Frequency of inorganic h […]

ll is generally written lly, sometimes ll […] Often ll designates simple ll […] Rarely l designates ll […]

nny designates nn, the same as lly, ll […] A single time, simple n designates nn: conpanero 32.4.

Vacillation between s and ss […] Sometimes ss is found following consonants […]

Vacillation between segund 2.1,3, 4.9, etc., segunt 1.4,6, 5.8, etc.; end 1.8, 10.28, en 5.6, 12.15.

Duplication of other consonants is observed in maniffestar 9.16, specifficadament 12.29, certifficar 36.24, arracon 9.1; following a consonant honrrado 32.3, comppradores 13.2; simple r is seen for rr in encorido 11.8.

Elision of the e in de is observed a few times: d’ ella 30.3, d’ ellos 27.10, 30.4 (but de ellos 27.8), d’ aquiadelant 26.12, d’ aquienant 16.5, 19.2 (but de aqui enant 20.1), d’ est 37.5, d’ esto 36.65, d’ otras 12.11 (but de aquel 21.6, 26.17, etc.). Elision of the e of the relative que is noted in 12.11 (qu’ en), elision of the a in cadanno 6.2, por aduçir 9.1, a pagar 16.12, l’ aljama 21.7, 30.2, apocope of the dative le 12.15 and of the o in ciento 36.2.

Patterns

The most common name pattern is ⟨given name⟩ + ⟨surname⟩, with 46 examples.

There are seven examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ son of (don) ⟨given⟩:

  • Acach Alitienz, fillyo de don Ybraym
  • Açahc Trigo fillyo de don Açahc
  • Azmel Churchullu fillo de Salamon (probably the son of Sallamon Churchullu)
  • Bidal Abenaçora fillyo de don Açach
  • Gento Auenbitas fillyo de don Mosse
  • Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach
  • Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda

Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach and Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda are the only case where the patronymic is necessary to distinguish between people in the document.

There are three examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ + ⟨junior/senior⟩: Bitas Ffalleua el Menor [the Younger] (Bitas Fallyeua is later in the same list), Salamon Abnarabj el Menor [the Younger] (Salamon Abnarrabj is earlier in the same list) and Salamon Auenllopiel el Mayor [the Elder] (no other Salamon Auenllopiel appears in the document).

There are two examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨profession⟩, both referring to the same person: Barcellya, corredor publico [public broker] and Barçellya, notario [notary].

There is one example of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨locative⟩: Faro de Granada.

Rabi seems to function as a title in this document. The men identified as rabbis are Rabj Mosse Aramo, Rabj Açach Jaba, don Rabi Dauid (no other bynames), and Rabi Simuel Jaba.

Surnames
