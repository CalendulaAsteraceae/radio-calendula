Three people are mentioned multiple times, and I count every instance of their names separately: Barcellya, corredor publico = Barçellya, notario; Bitas Faleua = Bitas Fallyeua; Salamon Abnarrabj = Salamon Abnarrabj.

There are a few individuals who I think are not Jewish. Except for the list of place names, their names are not included in the following lists, but their names are still interesting (and my identification could be incorrect):

  • Domingo Pereç de Mallyen, notario publico de la ciudat de Çaragoça (1 instance)
  • Garcia Aznarez delos nauarros (1 instance)
  • Guillyem Veguer, clerigo missacantano (1 instance)
  • Steuan Gil (4 instances) = Steuan Gil Tarin (2 instances)

Header forms are attested names, with i/j and u/v swaps to follow modern expectations that these be separate letters. The Aben- surnames all use b for consistency.

Patterns

The most common name pattern is ⟨given name⟩ + ⟨surname⟩, with 46 examples.

There are seven examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ son of (don) ⟨given⟩:

  • Acach Alitienz, fillyo de don Ybraym
  • Açahc Trigo fillyo de don Açahc
  • Azmel Churchullu fillo de Salamon (probably the son of Sallamon Churchullu)
  • Bidal Abenaçora fillyo de don Açach
  • Gento Auenbitas fillyo de don Mosse
  • Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach
  • Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda

Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach and Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda are the only case where the patronymic is necessary to distinguish between people in the document.

There are three examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ + ⟨junior/senior⟩: Bitas Ffalleua el Menor [the Younger] (Bitas Fallyeua is later in the same list), Salamon Abnarabj el Menor [the Younger] (Salamon Abnarrabj is earlier in the same list) and Salamon Auenllopiel el Mayor [the Elder] (no other Salamon Auenllopiel appears in the document).

There are two examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨profession⟩, both referring to the same person: Barcellya, corredor publico [public broker] and Barçellya, notario [notary].

There is one example of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨locative⟩: Faro de Granada.

Rabi seems to function as a title in this document. The men identified as rabbis are Rabj Mosse Aramo, Rabj Açach Jaba, don Rabi Dauid (no other bynames), and Rabi Simuel Jaba.

Surnames

The surnames beginning with Aben- are likely derived from literal patronyms of the form ben ⟨given⟩, but appear to have become inherited surnames. There are two individuals identified with both an Aben- surname and a literal patronymic: Bidal Abenaçora fillyo de don Açach and Gento Auenbitas fillyo de don Mosse.
