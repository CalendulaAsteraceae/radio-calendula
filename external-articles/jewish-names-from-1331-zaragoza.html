Three people are mentioned multiple times, usually with their name spelled differently each time, and I'm counting all of the names: Barcellya, corredor publico/Barçellya, notario, Bitas Faleua/Bitas Fallyeua, Salamon Abnarrabj/Salamon Abnarrabj. List the probably-not-Jewish people and translate their names: Steuan Gil AKA Steuan Gil Tarin; Domingo Pereç de Mallyen, notario publico de la ciudat de Çaragoça; Garcia Aznarez delos nauarros, notario; Guillyem Veguer, clerigo missacantano.

Header forms are attested names, possibly with i/j and u/v swaps to follow modern expectations that these be separate letters. Also all the Aben- surnames use b for consistency.

Patterns

The most common name pattern is ⟨given name⟩ + ⟨surname⟩, with 46 examples.

There are seven examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ son of (don) ⟨given⟩: Acach Alitienz, fillyo de don Ybraym; Açahc Trigo fillyo de don Açahc; Azmel Churchullu fillo de Salamon (probably the son of Sallamon Churchullu); Bidal Abenaçora fillyo de don Açach; Gento Auenbitas fillyo de don Mosse; Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach; and Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda. Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Açach and Simuell Golluff fillyo de don Juda are the only case where the patronymic is necessary to distinguish between people in the document. Add translations.

There are three examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨surname⟩ + ⟨junior/senior⟩: Bitas Ffalleua el Menor (Bitas Fallyeua is later in the same list), Salamon Abnarabj el Menor (Salamon Abnarrabj is earlier in the same list) and Salamon Auenllopiel el Mayor (no other Salamon Auenllopiel appears in the document). Add translations

There are two examples of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨profession⟩, both referring to the same person: Barcellya, corredor publico and Barçellya, notario. Add translations.

One example of ⟨given⟩ + ⟨locative⟩: Faro de Granada.

One example of given name with no byname, if Rabi is considered a title and not a profession: Rabi Dauid. The other identified rabbis are Rabj Mosse Aramo, Rabj Açach Jaba and Rabi Simuel Jaba.

Surnames

The surnames beginning with Aben- are likely derived from literal patronyms of the form ben ⟨given⟩, but appear to have become inherited surnames. There are two individuals identified with both an Aben- surname and a literal patronymic: Bidal Abenaçora fillyo de don Açach and Gento Auenbitas fillyo de don Mosse.
